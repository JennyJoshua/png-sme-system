## 1. Executive Summary

PNG already has a meaningful but uneven SME-support ecosystem: IPA provides online business registration and registry services; SME Corporation provides training, advisory, formalisation and finance-access support; banks and mobile-money providers provide payments infrastructure; and several local and international vendors sell POS, inventory, ERP and accounting software. [page:31][web:16][web:19][web:20][web:21][web:23]

The most important conclusion is that **generic “SME software” is not an obvious gap**. PNGPOS already sells an Android-oriented POS/inventory product with offline selling, supplier management and reporting from K75/month; Cloudcode’s Wantok POS advertises offline-first operations; and international POS/billing vendors market PNG-specific packages. [web:23][web:27][web:21][web:29][web:30] A new generic inventory or POS app would therefore face direct commercial competition and would need a sharply differentiated wedge.

By contrast, the evidence continues to point to **service-heavy, workflow-specific gaps** rather than another general business app:

- **Customer-credit and receivables management** remains poorly served by generic POS products, which focus on sales, stock and reporting rather than informal credit books, partial payments and collection follow-up. **HYPOTHESIS / COMMERCIAL VALIDATION REQUIRED.**
- **Loan-readiness and practical bookkeeping** may complement rather than duplicate SME Corporation’s training and formalisation support, which covers registration, business profiles and basic financial management but does not appear to offer ongoing, paid, owner-level bookkeeping or lender-ready record preparation. **PARTIALLY DOCUMENTED / FIELD VALIDATION REQUIRED.** [page:31][web:22]
- **Quotation, job-costing and job-record support** for contractors, tradespeople and mechanics is not visibly addressed by the POS-oriented products found. **HYPOTHESIS / FIELD VALIDATION REQUIRED.**
- **Supplier and freight coordination** remains a plausible but high-operational-cost opportunity; no strong PNG-specific marketplace or freight-coordination provider was verified in this pass. **HYPOTHESIS / UNKNOWN.**
- **Tender-readiness and business-document services** may complement SME Corporation advisory work, but demand depends on access to real procurement pipelines. **HYPOTHESIS / FIELD VALIDATION REQUIRED.**

The strongest immediate validation candidates are **credit/receivables management** and **quotation/job-record support**, because both can be tested manually with low startup cost, target a clear recurring pain point, and do not directly duplicate the POS/inventory products already marketed in PNG. [web:23][web:21]

## 4. PNG SME Digital Hub Assessment

**Overall assessment: PARTIALLY DOCUMENTED / UNKNOWN.** The Hub could not be retrieved during this research pass, so its current services, eligibility rules, fees, geographic coverage and demonstrated outcomes could not be verified from the primary site. [page:31]

### 4.1 What requires verification

| Claimed/possible service | What must be verified | Current evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| National SME registration and database | Whether businesses can register online, what data is collected, who owns the data, whether registration is free, and whether the database is live | UNKNOWN |
| Business visibility/listings | Whether listings are public, searchable, maintained and used by buyers | UNKNOWN |
| Access to finance/lender connections | Whether lenders actively use the database; whether referrals lead to loans | UNKNOWN |
| Business websites | Whether website creation is free, paid, templated, maintained and actually delivered | UNKNOWN |
| Digital marketing support | Whether support is advisory only or includes managed services | UNKNOWN |
| Business training | Whether training is current, free, accredited, available outside Port Moresby and measured for outcomes | UNKNOWN |
| Field-based registration/enumerators | Where enumeration occurs, how many businesses are registered, data quality and follow-up | UNKNOWN |
| Relationship with SMEC, GGGI, Australian programmes, eDOS | Formal governance, funding, data-sharing and operational responsibility | UNKNOWN |

### 4.2 What the Hub does **not** appear to establish

Even if the Hub successfully registers businesses and creates listings, public evidence would still be needed to show that it:

- Produces loan approvals.
- Produces contract awards.
- Generates customer sales.
- Maintains accurate and current business data.
- Provides ongoing bookkeeping, credit management, quotation or job-management support.
- Reaches rural and district-town businesses sustainably.

**Do not assume** that inclusion in a national SME database gives a business access to finance, procurement or customers. That causal chain requires separate evidence.

### 4.3 Strategic implication

An independent service should initially **avoid duplicating** registration, basic listings and general SME information. If the Hub is operational, the more plausible positioning is complementary:

- Help businesses maintain the records that make a Hub profile, loan application or tender pack credible.
- Provide workflow-specific services the Hub does not appear to offer, such as credit tracking or quotation/job management.
- Use Hub registration as one input to a broader business-readiness service, subject to data-sharing and consent.

**FIELD VALIDATION REQUIRED:** Interview businesses that have used the Hub to establish what they actually received, whether it was free, and whether it changed any commercial outcome.

## 5. SME Corporation Programmes

### 5.1 Mandate and current services

**DOCUMENTED:** SME Corporation is an implementing agency under the Ministry of Commerce and Industry, mandated through the SME Corporation Act 2014 to drive MSME development, formalisation and private-sector growth. Its stated work includes capacity building, business formalisation and financial access. [page:31]

**DOCUMENTED:** SME Corporation advertises entrepreneurship training, including “Start Your Business” (SYB), covering business planning, marketing, record-keeping and basic financial management. It also states that it helps trainees register business names with IPA and currently covers 75% of IPA registration cost for participants, subject to terms and conditions. [web:22]

**DOCUMENTED:** SME Corporation describes a formalisation pathway: register the business name with IPA, obtain a TIN from IRC, compile a business profile, and open a business bank account. It says it is ready to assist MSMEs willing to register. [web:22]

**DOCUMENTED:** Planned/implemented impact areas from 2019–2025 include MSME training and capacity building, MSME incubation centres, an MSME database and baseline survey, and establishment of an SME Development Fund. [page:31]

### 5.2 What is unclear

| Area | Unknown / requires verification |
|---|---|
| Training availability | Current schedule, locations, fees, class sizes, completion rates and post-training business outcomes |
| Incubation centres | Which centres are operational, where, who is eligible, and what services they provide |
| SME Development Fund | Current status, disbursement criteria, approval rates and whether it is accepting applications |
| Financial-access support | Whether SMEC provides loan packaging, introductions, guarantees or only guidance |
| Rural reach | Whether services are delivered outside Port Moresby and major centres, and at what scale |
| Database | Whether the MSME database is public, current and used by lenders or buyers |
| Registration subsidy | Current eligibility, duration, funding availability and process |

### 5.3 Overlap and complementarity

SME Corporation already addresses:

- Entrepreneurship training.
- Basic business management and record-keeping awareness.
- Business-name registration guidance and partial registration-cost support.
- Business formalisation guidance.
- General financial-access support. [page:31][web:22]

This means an independent service should **not** lead with:

- Generic “how to start a business” training.
- Basic business-name registration information.
- A general SME directory or awareness portal.

It may complement SMEC by providing:

- Ongoing, practical bookkeeping and cash-flow records.
- Customer-credit ledgers and collection support.
- Quotation, invoice and job documentation.
- Loan-file preparation that turns training and registration into usable financial records.
- Sector-specific operational support not covered by generic training.

## 6. Other Government and Development Programmes

### 6.1 Investment Promotion Authority

**DOCUMENTED:** IPA administers PNG’s business registry, covering companies, business names, business groups and associations. Its registry is online, publicly searchable, and supports online registration and filings; after the initial application, filings must be done online, with payment through BSP Online, card, or cash/cheque at BSP. [web:16][web:18][web:19]

**Implication:** Basic business registration is already digitised. A new service should not rebuild registration infrastructure. It could instead help owners navigate documentation, maintain records and prepare post-registration compliance or finance materials—subject to verification of actual owner pain points.

### 6.2 Internal Revenue Commission

**PARTIALLY DOCUMENTED:** IRC is part of SME Corporation’s described formalisation sequence through TIN issuance. [web:22] Its current tax obligations, digital services and SME-facing guidance were not directly inspected in this pass and must be verified from official IRC sources before any tax-related service is designed.

### 6.3 Banks and payments

**DOCUMENTED:** BSP publishes merchant fees including EFTPOS, internet payment gateway, BSP Pay and mobile merchant services. BSP Pay is listed at PGK 0.25 per transaction for micro-SMEs, while EFTPOS local-card transactions are listed at 1%. [web:20]

**Implication:** Payment acceptance is commercially available, but fees, terminal requirements, settlement processes and customer cash preferences still matter. A business service should integrate with—not replace—existing payment rails.

### 6.4 Development partners

**PARTIALLY DOCUMENTED:** The first-stage report documents UNCDF-supported work on women’s digital and financial capabilities, including the CellMoni pilot, and broader development-partner attention to financial inclusion and digital adoption. [web:5][web:10] These initiatives address inclusion and capability but do not appear to provide ongoing, paid operational management for individual SMEs.

## 11. Potential Complementary Opportunities

1. **Credit-book clean-up and weekly receivables tracking.** Start as a human service using a spreadsheet or simple form; add reminders and reporting only after paid repeat use.
2. **Quote-to-cash support for trades.** Standard quotation template, material/labour cost checklist, invoice and payment follow-up.
3. **Loan-file preparation service.** Convert basic sales/expense/stock records into a lender-ready pack; validate requirements with lenders first.
4. **Sector-specific record kits.** E.g., mechanic job cards, hardware quote books, transport trip sheets—delivered with brief setup assistance.
5. **Managed weekly business review.** A human operator consolidates sales, expenses, stock and debts into a one-page summary.
6. **Supplier-sourcing concierge.** For one narrow category, such as building materials or agricultural inputs, before any marketplace build.
7. **Freight concierge.** For one route or commodity, with clear liability limits and verified carrier relationships.

## 14. Priority Questions for Business Owners

1. When a customer buys on credit, how exactly do you record it, and what happens when they pay part of the amount?
2. How much money is currently owed to you, how old is the oldest debt, and how often do you fail to collect?
3. How do you know whether last week was profitable?
4. What records did you use for your last loan application, and what did the lender ask for?
5. Tell me about the last quotation you prepared: how long did it take, what information did you need, and did you win the job?
6. How do you currently find out a supplier’s price and whether stock is available?
7. What happened the last time a delivery was late, damaged, lost or more expensive than expected?
8. Which administrative task would you most happily pay someone else to do?
9. Have you ever paid an accountant, consultant, staff member or relative to manage records, quotes, collections or procurement?
10. What would make you trust a new person or service with your business records?

## 15. Proposed Field-Validation Plan

### 15.1 Low-cost validation sequence

1. **Week 1–2: 10 owner interviews.** Use the first-stage interview plan, with added credit-book, quotation and supplier questions.
2. **Week 2–3: artefact review.** With consent, review the structure—not necessarily confidential values—of credit books, quote books, stock records and loan documents.
3. **Week 3–4: paid concierge pilot.** Choose one of:
   - Credit-book clean-up + weekly receivables summary.
   - Five quotation/job-record packs for tradespeople.
   - Ten supplier-sourcing requests.
4. **Charge a small real fee.** Free pilots do not establish demand.
5. **Measure behaviour, not stated interest:** repeat purchase, referral, completion rate, time saved, errors avoided, and whether the owner would pay again.

### 15.2 Suggested pilot: credit and receivables

- Recruit 5–10 trade stores, transport operators or service firms.
- Reconstruct their customer-credit book into a simple, private ledger.
- Produce a weekly aging summary and follow-up list.
- Charge a modest setup fee and a short weekly service fee.
- Success signals: owner continues after the first cycle, refers another owner, and reports recovered payments or reduced disputes.
- Failure signals: owner will not share data, sees no value after clean-up, or will not pay for ongoing tracking.

## 16. Recommendations

1. **Do not build a generic POS, inventory or SME-directory product.** Direct competitors and public services already occupy that space. [web:23][web:21][web:16][page:31]
2. **Prioritise field validation of credit/receivables management and quote-to-cash support.** Both are service-testable, low-capital and potentially complementary to existing systems.
3. **Validate loan-readiness demand with lenders before selling it to SMEs.** SMEC already covers formalisation guidance and training; the unresolved question is whether better ongoing records change lending outcomes.
4. **Treat supplier and freight coordination as concierge experiments,** not platform builds.
5. **Use the PNG SME Digital Hub only after direct verification.** Its current services, reach and outcomes could not be confirmed in this pass. [page:31]
6. **Use manual delivery first:** spreadsheets, messaging, templates, phone calls and a simple mobile-friendly form are sufficient to test demand.
7. **Do not claim offline-first capability** unless the essential workflow demonstrably functions without connectivity; PNGPOS and Wantok make such claims, but a new service should not copy the label without the operational design. [web:23][web:21]

## 18. Research Limitations

1. **PNG SME Digital Hub was not accessible** during this research pass; no first-hand verification of its services, fees, eligibility, coverage or outcomes was possible. [page:31]
2. **No GitHub commit was made** because repository write access was not available in this environment.

4. **Product adoption is unverified.** Vendor websites establish availability and pricing claims, not market usage.
5. **Credit losses, quotation conversion losses, stock-out costs and freight failure costs are not quantified** for PNG.
6. **IRC, NDB, provincial administrations and many development programmes were not exhaustively reviewed** in this pass.
7. **Informal-workaround prevalence is inferred**, not measured.
8. **No revenue forecast, market-size estimate or profit projection is included**, because the necessary demand and pricing evidence does not yet exist.
